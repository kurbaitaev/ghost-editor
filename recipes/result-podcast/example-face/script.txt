You walk into the kitchen and forget why you came.
You just stand there, staring at the fridge.
Your memory is fine. Blame the door.
Psychologists call it the doorway effect.
Your brain saves the day in scenes, like a film.
A doorway tells it: new scene, file the old one away.
And the plan you had goes into that file.
In experiments, people forgot more after walking through a door than after walking the same distance in one room.
So before you go, say the task out loud: scissors, scissors, scissors.
Now it walks through the door with you.
Doors close scenes. They don't have to close your plans.
